CREATIVE DIRECTION

Style: elegant, vibrant, and richly festive, with a premium,
royal feel appropriate to a high-end retail supplier of plywood,
timber, laminates, doors and hardware.

You may use either a photorealistic scene or a polished graphic /
illustrated design (flat colors, ornamental line art, iconography)
— whichever best suits a colorful, celebratory festival greeting.
Favor rich, vibrant colors and elegant ornamental detail over a
muted or purely photographic look.

{style_spark}

Feel free to experiment with different layouts — centered,
top-to-bottom, symmetrical, or medallion-style arrangements —
rather than always placing text on the left and imagery on the
right.

Beyond that, use your own creative judgment for lighting, mood,
color palette, and composition. Vary your creative choices
naturally from poster to poster rather than repeating the same
layout every time.

OBJECTIVE

Wish and connect with customers first.

Tie the festive greeting naturally to the business (wood, doors, home, craftsmanship) without being overly salesy.

The design should make the business look trustworthy, premium, and culturally warm.

Festival mood and imagery should be the dominant visual focus of
the poster. The decorations, colors, motifs, and symbolic elements
shown must be authentic and specific to the exact festival named
in the topic above.

Do not default to one festival's visual style for every occasion.
For example: diyas, rangoli, and marigold garlands suit Diwali or
Navratri; a modak and Ganesha idol suit Ganesh Chaturthi; a peacock
feather and flute suit Janmashtami; lanterns and crescent-moon
motifs suit Eid; ornaments, pine, and warm string lights suit
Christmas; colored powders suit Holi. Let the specific festival in
the topic determine which symbols, colors, and decorations
actually appear — never mix elements from a different festival
than the one named.

You may optionally include a very small, unobtrusive text list of
product categories or a single subtle icon — but feel free to
leave it out entirely if it would distract from the festive
design. Most posters should lean fully into the celebration with
little to no product presence.

IMPORTANT

Do NOT include:

• Company logo
• Company name
• Phone number
• Website
• Footer
• QR code
• Social media handles
• Watermarks

A small logo and contact details will be added afterward in one
corner of the image, and should not be part of your design.

Try to keep at least one corner of the composition relatively
uncluttered — not empty or artificial-looking, just naturally
calmer than the rest of the scene — since that's where the brand
mark will be placed. Beyond that, compose freely.

Use high-quality, polished imagery — photorealistic or illustrated, whichever fits the chosen style.

Avoid generic stock-photo festival clichés — aim for a design that feels custom-made and premium, not a template.
